# OXPHOS dysfunction drives metabolic remodeling in tumor-associated macrophages

## Abstract

Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) reprogram their metabolism to survive in the hypoxic tumor core. Here we show that OXPHOS deficiency remodels the epigenetic landscape of TAMs. Using single-cell RNA sequencing of 320 patients, we identify a mitochondrial-stress-associated macrophage state that predicts immunotherapy response.

## Introduction

The tumor microenvironment imposes metabolic stress on infiltrating immune cells. Macrophages display remarkable metabolic plasticity during polarization. Mitochondrial dysfunction has been implicated in inflammatory activation. However, the in vivo relevance of these pathways remains poorly characterized. Single-cell approaches now allow dissection of metabolic states at unprecedented resolution. We therefore combined metabolomics with single-cell transcriptomics in patient cohorts.

## Results

### OXPHOS deficiency defines a distinct macrophage state

We surveyed 320 patients with colorectal cancer, of whom 180 received anti-PD1 therapy, while 200 received chemotherapy alone. Single-cell profiling revealed a cluster of macrophages with depressed electron transport chain gene expression. These cells exhibited impaired spare respiratory capacity measured by Seahorse extracellular flux analysis with p=0.000 across all three independent cohorts. The metabolic shift correlated with reduced histone acetylation at promoter regions of inflammatory loci.

### Metabolic remodeling predicts therapy response

Patients enriched for the mitochondrial-stress macrophage state showed poorer progression-free survival. The association remained significant after covariate adjustment in multivariate models. Figure 4 summarizes the survival analysis across cohorts.

## Discussion

Patients enriched for the mitochondrial-stress macrophage state showed poorer progression-free survival. Our findings reposition macrophage metabolism as a actionable vulnerability in oncology. The single-cell metabolomic axis completely revolutionizes cancer immunotherapy stratification for patients with colorectal cancer treated with immune checkpoint blockade in the first-line setting. Mitochondrial stress in macrophages therefore merits prospective validation as a biomarker in larger and more diverse cohorts with longer follow-up duration. Therapeutic interventions that restore electron transport flux might reverse the suppressive phenotype and synergize with existing immunotherapeutic approaches.

## Methods

Patients were recruited under IRB-approved protocols. Single-cell suspensions were profiled on the 10x Genomics platform. Statistical analyses were performed in R version 4.3.
